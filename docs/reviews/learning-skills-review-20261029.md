## What I need to do differently

The owner's target is a coherent, convincing, explorable world. I need to treat
construction, texture scale, object proportions, sight lines, interaction and
movement as one player experience, and choose evidence that can expose its
defects. Safe authority boundaries and correct file formats support that target;
they do not finish it.

1. **Derive construction and UVs together.** The repeated wall/floor seams,
   lips, stair overlaps and corner notches called for shared finished-face
   datums, deliberate joint ownership and a coherent texture pitch. My next
   correction must start by classifying the seam and measuring neighboring faces.
   Adding trim is not evidence that the underlying math is correct. The owner
   invoked block-building intuition for alignment and construction logic, without
   requesting mandatory voxels or Minecraft dimensions.
2. **Make connected stairs visibly understandable.** An ordinary visible stair
   should show where it leads. The owner explicitly relaxed the earlier
   discoverability framing: directly connected room sections above/below need
   bounded structural sight, including both directions when present. Implement
   that through the lawful projection, while keeping loot/occupants, inspected
   knowledge and movement authority distinct. A prior narrow projection must not
   become a design excuse for black boundaries or invisible connections.
3. **Calibrate usable models around the actor.** The avatar was correct; books
   were too large. Five-to-six rows per person was a reference for plausible
   ordinary books, not a universal numeric rule. A cabinet must meet its frame
   and respond to the offered controls. Support beams need purpose, contact and
   clearance. Test actual pivots, operating states and the real player collider;
   preserve the distinction between visual cabinet poses and inventory systems.
4. **Include camera and motion in playability.** Auto should walk in steady flow
   with a useful travel-facing camera, while presenting game-owned accepted
   movement. Content revisions should not restart a step or cause needless
   flicker/reallocation. Qualify elapsed-time motion, turning, stair transitions,
   Stop/control handoff, texture remounts and resource lifetime in the actual
   host. Old local performance samples are evidence about their exact runs.
5. **Look from the view that can falsify the claim.** My initial downward stair
   capture showed treads, so it could not prove the riser treatment. The later
   upward capture did. The same discipline applies to moving-camera z-fighting,
   oblique cabinet fit and humanoid front/back/side UVs. The owner should not
   repeatedly have to discover defects outside the views I chose. Green tests,
   a loaded atlas and an attractive workshop pose remain partial evidence.
6. **Carry corrections through integration.** The owner observed rails and old
   stair defects return during base alignment. Before the next rebase, retain
   the semantic acceptance checklist and compare actual behavior afterward.
   Preserve isolated worktrees, committed checkpoints, source pins and asset
   reproduction steps. A clean merge does not prove the repaired experience
   survived. Current generator/base changes still need their own reconciliation.

Two producer lessons also matter. First, the glowstone work tested whether the
upgrade process was good enough; manual retouching would have changed that test.
The separately authorized bookshelf repair had a different target and should
retain its repair provenance. Second, repeated asset refreshes need stable IDs,
useful names and source provenance, with revisions separate from NPC/class
identity. A partially complete run is not permission to import everything.
These extend existing selected-intake and pixel-integrity guidance rather than
contradicting it.
